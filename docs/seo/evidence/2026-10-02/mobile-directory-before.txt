    - paragraph: "Practical memorial donation wording for an obituary: in lieu of flowers examples, charity details to verify, donation-link safety, family-support language, and thank-you tracking."
    - region "Quick Answer":
      - heading "Quick Answer" [level=2]
      - paragraph: "The clearest memorial donation wording names the person, the organization, the official donation path, and how the gift should be designated. A simple version is: In memory of [Name], memorial donations may be made to [Organization Name] at [official website or mailing address]."
      - paragraph: Before publishing, verify the charity name, donation link, mailing address, family-notification process, and whether the request is for a registered charity, memorial fund, scholarship, or family support. Those are different gift paths, and the obituary should make the difference clear.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: Does in lieu of flowers mean no flowers?
        - text: It expresses the family’s preference for a donation.
        - link "Emily Post’s funeral etiquette guidance":
          - /url: https://emilypost.com/advice/funeral-etiquette-donations-in-lieu-of-flowers
        - text: says flowers may accompany a contribution; when choosing one tribute, follow the family’s request. State a no-flowers preference explicitly rather than relying on the phrase alone.
    - region "Donation Language You Can Adapt":
      - generic: Wording examples
      - heading "Donation Language You Can Adapt" [level=2]
      - heading "Simple charity request" [level=3]
      - paragraph: In memory of [Name], memorial donations may be made to [Organization Name] at [official website or mailing address].
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this when the family has chosen one official charity or memorial-giving page.
      - heading "Gentler in-lieu wording" [level=3]
      - paragraph: If you wish to honor [Name]'s memory, please consider a gift to [Organization Name], a cause close to [his/her/their] heart.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this when the phrase in lieu of flowers feels too blunt, but the family still wants to guide gifts toward a cause.
      - heading "Flowers or donations both welcome" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Flowers are welcome, or memorial gifts may be directed to [Organization Name] in [Name]'s memory.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this when some relatives may want to send flowers and others may prefer a donation.
      - heading "Favorite charity option" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Those wishing to remember [Name] may make a donation to a charity meaningful to them, noting the gift in [Name]'s memory.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this when the family does not want to name one organization or when the person's interests were broad.
      - heading "Scholarship or memorial fund" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Memorial contributions may be made to the [Fund Name], established to support [purpose]. Details are available at [official link].
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this only after the fund, school, community foundation, or fiscal sponsor is ready to receive gifts.
      - heading "Family support or funeral expenses" [level=3]
      - paragraph: For those who wish to help, contributions toward funeral and family expenses may be made through [official fundraiser or funeral-home link].
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Use when:"
        - text: Use this when support is going to the family rather than a registered charity, and say that clearly.
    - region "Wording For A Donation Made In Someone’s Memory":
      - heading "Wording For A Donation Made In Someone’s Memory" [level=2]
      - paragraph: An obituary asks others to give; a donor’s card tells the family that a gift has already been made. Use the examples above for the request. For a card or personal note after donating, name the person you are remembering and the organization that received the gift.
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "Simple donor note:"
        - text: “I have made a gift to [Organization Name] in remembrance of [Name]. I’m thinking of you and your family.”
      - paragraph:
        - strong: "With a personal memory:"
        - text: “I’ll always remember [Name]’s kindness when [brief memory]. I have made a memorial gift to [Organization Name] to honor that kindness.”
      - paragraph:
        - text: These are original examples to adapt after the donation is complete.
        - link "St. Jude’s memorial-card guidance":
          - /url: https://www.stjude.org/give/memorials-and-dedications/memorial-donation-wording.html
        - text: recommends making the message personal, identifying the cause, and proofreading the names. Check whether the charity will send its own notification so you know what the family will receive.
    - region "Details To Confirm First":
      - generic: Before publishing
      - heading "Details To Confirm First" [level=2]
      - article:
        - heading "Use the official organization name" [level=3]
        - paragraph: Write the charity name exactly as the organization uses it. Avoid abbreviations that could point donors to the wrong nonprofit.
      - article:
        - heading "Add one verified giving path" [level=3]
        - paragraph: Use the charity's official memorial-giving link, tribute page, phone number, or mailing address. If the link is long, use the funeral home or memorial page as the single source of truth.
      - article:
        - heading "Say how to designate the gift" [level=3]
        - paragraph: Tell donors whether to write the person's name in an online memorial field, check memo line, card message, or tribute form.
      - article:
        - heading "Check family notification" [level=3]
        - paragraph: Many charities can send a notice to the family without sharing the gift amount. Confirm what donor and family information is needed before publishing.
      - article:
        - heading "Separate charity gifts from family help" [level=3]
        - paragraph: If money is going to funeral expenses, a spouse, children, or a household, do not describe it as a charity donation. Use plain family-support language.
      - article:
        - heading "Keep records after publication" [level=3]
        - paragraph: Save the obituary text, donation link, charity contact, donor notices, and thank-you list so the family can acknowledge gifts later.
    - region "Avoid These Donation-Wording Mistakes":
      - heading "Avoid These Donation-Wording Mistakes" [level=2]
      - list:
        - listitem: Do not publish an unverified donation link, shortened URL, or third-party fundraiser that the family has not approved.
        - listitem: Do not name a charity before confirming it can receive memorial gifts in the person's name.
        - listitem: Do not imply donations are mandatory; the wording should guide people, not pressure them.
        - listitem: Do not mix up flowers, Mass cards, family-expense gifts, and charity donations in one unclear sentence.
        - listitem: Do not list multiple organizations without checking spelling, addresses, and whether each link is official.
        - listitem: Do not promise tax treatment in the obituary. Let the charity provide receipts and tax language.
